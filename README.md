# CS255

**Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
**
The DriverPass project was based around making a website that will have three different packages that teach students how to drive, also have scheduled appointments that have students meet up with driving instructors, and have a direct connection to the DMV database to keep up with any updates. 

**What did you do particularly well?
**
I believe I did the activity charts and Gantt charts pretty well. I am more of a planning type of person who likes to see the plan and flow laid out, so I think I excelled in that part of the project.

However, I often get a bit jumbled and confused when making them myself because i don't know when there should be classes of their own. I also want to strengthen this skill to eventually understand the coding that should be added to the class UML as well. 

**How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
**
I like to htink of things like I am actually in the place of the User. I tried to understand parts of the project that weren't outrightly demanded in the project like having the account freeze after three attempts rather than immediately after one mistake, because there are times when humans can make mistakes with a case-sensitive password. 

**How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
**
I am very intimidated by the job of designing and developing a software. I think the first steps are definitely to have a secure plan and idea of what is wanted from the user. The clearer and more detailed an expectation is, the easier it is to deliver. I think having frequent meetings among a team is necessary to keep the project in a productive flow and on the same playing field. I would make use of UMLs to have the team understand how the project is meant to look and the thought process behind the system.
